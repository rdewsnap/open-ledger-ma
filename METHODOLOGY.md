# Methodology

Open Ledger MA shows where Massachusetts state government money comes from and where it goes,
using public data published by the Office of the Comptroller on CTHRU. This page explains where
every number comes from, how the categories were built, and what the numbers do not show.

The code that produces every number is in [`/etl`](etl/). Run `make all` to reproduce the data
from scratch.

## Sources

| What | Dataset | ID | Links | Filter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spending (budgeted and spent) | CTHRU Budget-to-Actual, "Budget_Actual_With_Other_Spending_Authorizationv3_Gen2" | `kv7m-35wn` | [dataset page](https://cthru.data.socrata.com/d/kv7m-35wn) · [API](https://cthru.data.socrata.com/resource/kv7m-35wn.json) | none |
| Revenue (collected) | CTHRU "Commonwealth Revenue Collections" | `kcy7-ivxi` | [dataset page](https://cthru.data.socrata.com/d/kcy7-ivxi) · [API](https://cthru.data.socrata.com/resource/kcy7-ivxi.json) | `fund_category_name = 'Budgeted Funds'` |
| Capital spending (shown beside the charts, not in them) | CTHRU "Comptroller of the Commonwealth Spending" | `pegc-naaa` | [dataset page](https://cthru.data.socrata.com/d/pegc-naaa) · [API](https://cthru.data.socrata.com/resource/pegc-naaa.json) | `appropriation_type like '(2CN)%'` |

All three datasets are on the Socrata portal at `cthru.data.socrata.com` and are pulled with the
SODA API. The data is refreshed automatically every Monday: a scheduled job
([`.github/workflows/refresh-data.yml`](.github/workflows/refresh-data.yml)) pulls every year
again, rebuilds the files, runs the checks, and publishes only if they pass. The date of the
latest pull, and each dataset's `rowsUpdatedAt` timestamp at that time, are in `data/meta.json`
(`sources.*.pulled`, `sources.*.rowsUpdatedAt`) and shown at the bottom of each chart page.

Any program on the site can be traced to its source rows with a query such as
`https://cthru.data.socrata.com/resource/kv7m-35wn.json?fiscal_year=2026&appropriation_account_number=40000500`.

Most named programs also link to their page on the state's budget site,
[budget.digital.mass.gov](https://budget.digital.mass.gov/summary/fy27/). That page shows the line
item's legal wording and its amount at each stage: the governor's proposal, the House and Senate
versions, the conference committee, any veto or override, and the final budget. The links come from
the site's sitemap; no number on this site is taken from those pages. Items the state site has no
page for, such as trust funds and items added only in supplemental budgets, have no link.

Reference only (not used for any number): the MassBudget Budget Browser category tree, used to
sanity-check how line items are grouped.

## What the money columns mean

Each row of `kv7m-35wn` is one fiscal year, fund and appropriation account (line item).

| Label on the site | Field | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Budgeted** | `total_enacted_budget_appropriation` | The budget the Legislature passed: the General Appropriations Act (GAA) plus supplemental budgets passed during the year. CTHRU calls this "enacted". |
| GAA (in data files as `gaa`) | `original_enacted_budget` | The amount in the original General Appropriations Act only. |
| **Spent** | `total_expenses` | Expenses recorded against the account in the state accounting system. For FY2027, "Spent so far". |

### Why "Spent" can be larger than "Budgeted"

In every closed year the state spent more than the budget the Legislature passed
(FY2026: about $69.8B spent against $62.2B budgeted). Most of the gap is spending that is
allowed but never appears as a line in the budget. Some of it is programs that ran over.

Allowed, but outside the budget:

- **Spending required by other laws** (`other_statutorily_authorized_spending`, about $4.9B in
  FY2026). Some payments are set by standing law instead of the yearly budget. The largest is
  the annual payment into the Pension Liabilities Fund (account 1595-1010): about $4.3B spent in
  FY2026, with $0 budgeted.
- **Agencies spending fees they collect.** Some accounts ("retained revenue") may spend the
  money they bring in, up to a ceiling set in the budget.
- **Money carried over from earlier years.** Unspent balances from earlier budgets can be spent
  this year.
- **Double-counted internal payments.** When one agency pays another for shared services such
  as IT (accounts labelled "chargeback" or "internal service fund"), the same dollars appear
  once as each agency's expense.

Programs that ran over:

- **Spending above the budget line** (about $2.5B across 61 accounts in FY2026, as pulled). The
  largest is MassHealth managed care (4000-0500): $7.41B spent against $6.01B budgeted. The
  state pays the bills, and the Legislature usually covers the difference with a later
  supplemental budget, which is added to "Budgeted" once CTHRU records it.

The site shows both columns as reported and does not try to net these out.

## How spending is organized

The hierarchy is **Spending area → Department → Program**.

- **Spending area** is the Governor's cabinet secretariat (CTHRU's `cabinet_secretariat_name`), plus the
  independent offices such as the Judiciary and the Legislature. The site says "spending area" as the
  more familiar term. Names are shortened for readability in
  `etl/names.py` (for example "EXECUTIVE OFFICE FOR ADMINSTRATION & FINANCE" becomes
  "Administration & Finance", and "CTR/FAD ACCOUNTING ENTITIES" becomes "Statewide obligations",
  which holds pension, retiree-benefit and fund-to-fund transfers). The source's own spelling
  errors are corrected only in the display name.
- **Department** is CTHRU's `department_code` / `department_name`, with readable names in
  `etl/names.py`. Some codes are legacy: `DMR` is the Department of Developmental Services,
  `DSS` is the Department of Children & Families, `WEL` is the Department of Transitional
  Assistance, `FWE` is the Department of Fish & Game.
- **Program** is one appropriation account (an 8-digit line item number, shown as e.g.
  `4000-0500`). Plain-English program names are hand-written in
  [`etl/mapping.csv`](etl/mapping.csv), keyed by **account number, not name**, so the same
  program keeps the same name and ID across years even when CTHRU's account name changes or is
  truncated. The original CTHRU name is kept in the data files (`raw`) for traceability.

### One home per account

CTHRU sometimes records one account under more than one department. For example, Chapter 70
school aid (7061-0008) is appropriated to the Department of Elementary & Secondary Education
but the payments are recorded under the Department of Revenue. To show each program once, every
row of an account is added up under a single home department:

1. If the account is in `mapping.csv`, its `secretariat` (spending area) and `department` columns decide.
2. Otherwise, the department holding the most enacted dollars for that account across FY2024–FY2027
   (ties broken by spending).

This moves dollars between departments and spending areas relative to a raw CTHRU group-by, but
never changes any total. The `notes` column of `mapping.csv` lists the other departments an
account's rows were rolled up from.

### "Other" buckets

`mapping.csv` names the largest appropriation accounts: the top 200 by spending in each of
FY2024–FY2027, plus the top 200 by enacted amount in FY2027 (because FY2027 spending is
partial), about 300 accounts in total. Together they cover about 96–98% of spending in each year
(see the table above).

Every other account is added into one **"Other {Department}"** node per department (marked
`other: true`). Those buckets hold real line items: smaller administrative accounts,
small grant programs, earmarks, retained-revenue accounts and similar. The number of accounts
inside each bucket is stored as `accounts` in `tree.json`. Nothing is dropped.

On the Flow view, only the 40 largest named programs statewide are drawn; everything else in a
spending area is drawn as one **"Other {area}"** node. The full detail is always in the
Scale and Changes views.

## How revenue is grouped

Revenue comes from `kcy7-ivxi`, limited to `fund_category_name = 'Budgeted Funds'`. Rows are
assigned to seven groups by `etl/revenue_groups.py`, using these rules in order:

| Group | Rule (CTHRU category / class) | Classes seen FY2024–FY2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Income tax | class `TAXES INCOME` | Taxes Income |
| Sales & use tax | class `TAXES SALES AND USE` | Taxes Sales and Use |
| Corporate tax | class `TAXES BUSINESS CORP`, `TAXES BANKS`, `TAXES -INSURANCE` | Business corporations, banks, insurance |
| Other taxes | category `TAXES`, any other class | Motor fuel, estate, deeds, cigarette, alcohol, room occupancy, cannabis, public utility, miscellaneous, raffle/beano, county correction deeds, surcharges |
| Federal reimbursements | category `FEDERAL GRANTS AND REIMBURSE` | Federal reimbursement (operating), federal special projects, federal incentives |
| Fees & departmental | category `DEPARTMENTAL`, `ASSESSMENTS`, `CHARGEBACK REVENUES` | Fees, licenses and permits, fines, reimbursements for services, assessments, rents, court collections, lottery, sale of goods |
| Transfers & other | everything else | Operating transfers in, fringe benefit recovery, interest income, pension contributions, escheats, trust contributions, miscellaneous |

"Transfers & other" includes operating transfers between state funds (about $6.1B in FY2026),
which are not new money entering state government.

### Why revenue minus spending is not a surplus

The Flow view shows revenue collected next to spending, and in closed years revenue is
higher (FY2026: $73.2B taken in, $69.8B spent). That gap is not a surplus or a deficit. The
two totals come from different state reports that count money differently:

- **Revenue counts money moved between state funds.** "Transfers & other" includes about
  $6.1B in FY2026 of operating transfers from one state fund to another. That is not new money
  coming in.
- **Spending counts some payments twice.** When one agency pays another for shared services,
  both expenses are recorded (see "Why Spent can be larger than Budgeted" above).
- **Timing differs.** Revenue is recorded when it arrives and spending when it is paid, and in
  FY2027 the two reports are current through different dates.

The state's official surplus or deficit comes from its year-end financial report, which nets
these out. This site does not try to.

### Revenue bands show shares of the pool, not traced dollars

Money in the General Fund is fungible: a dollar of income tax is not earmarked for a specific
program. The Flow view's bands from revenue sources to spending areas are a **proportional
allocation**: each spending area's total is split across revenue groups in proportion to each
group's share of total budgeted-fund collections that year. They show the overall mix, not
where any specific dollar went. These links are marked `proportional: true` in `flows.json`.
Because the revenue column is split to fill the spending total, a revenue band's width shows its
share, not its dollar amount; when collections and spending differ (always, and most of all in a
year still in progress), the revenue column is stretched or squeezed to match.
(Some revenue is in fact dedicated by law, such as the income surtax for the Education and
Transportation Fund; the allocation does not reflect that.)

## Capital spending

The charts show the **operating** side of the state's finances: the annual budget and the
spending recorded against it. The state also spends money on **capital** projects (roads and
bridges, buildings, parks, land purchases, housing and IT) that is mostly borrowed through bonds
and paid out over several years. In FY2026 that was about $4.6B of payments, over half of it for
transportation.

Capital is kept out of the charts for three reasons. Adding it to spending would mean adding
bond proceeds to revenue, which makes "revenue minus spending" even harder to read. A bond
authorization is spent over many years, so one year's capital payments do not line up with one
year's budget. And the operating budget is the figure the Legislature votes on each year and the
one news coverage refers to.

Instead, a spending area's capital payments are shown next to it: in the Flow breakdown panel and
above the Scale chart when you zoom in. The figures are payments from capital appropriations
(`appropriation_type` `(2CN) CAPITAL`) in the Comptroller's spending dataset, summed by department.
Capital is paid from separate funds, so none of it is already counted in the operating figures.
Federal highway money spent through the Federal Highway Construction fund is included; most other
federal grants are not.

The **Put an amount in context** box divides an amount you type by an area's operating figure,
by the same plus its capital payments, and by all operating spending. It is arithmetic only and
takes no view on whether an amount is large. The amount is kept in the page URL (`?amt=65000000`)
so a comparison can be shared.

## Aid to cities and towns

The **Your town** page shows two payments the budget sets for every city, town and regional school
district: **Chapter 70 school aid** and **Unrestricted General Government Aid**. The amounts come
from Section 3 of each year's enacted budget, which the state publishes as a spreadsheet on
[budget.digital.mass.gov](https://budget.digital.mass.gov/summary/fy27/local-aid/). FY2025 through
FY2027 are included; the FY2024 file is in an older format the pipeline does not read.

- These are amounts in the original budget, not payments made during the year.
- A regional school district gets its school aid directly, so a town whose schools are regional
  shows little or no school aid of its own. The page lists the regional districts a town's
  students attend as residents of a member town, from the state education department's
  [enrollment by town](https://educationtocareer.data.mass.gov/d/8xyg-59b2) data.
- **Residents** are the Census Bureau's Vintage 2025
  [city and town estimates](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/datasets/2020-2025/cities/totals/sub-est2025_25.csv)
  for July 1, 2025, which add up to the state estimate used for per-resident figures. The county
  shown beside a town's name comes from the same file.
- **Students** are counted on October 1 of the latest school year in that enrollment data. For a
  city or town it is the public school students who live there, wherever they go to school (their
  own district, a regional district, a charter school or another town's schools). For a regional
  district it is the students enrolled in it. Private school and home-schooled students are not
  counted. The page does not divide aid by students, because a town's school aid does not cover
  the students its regional districts are paid for.
- Other local aid is not included: charter school reimbursements, Chapter 90 road money, library
  aid, school construction grants, and the charges the state bills back to a community. The
  Division of Local Services' [cherry sheets](https://www.mass.gov/cherry-sheet-estimates)
  give the full account.
- The unrestricted aid on the list adds up to its budget line item (1233-2350); `make check`
  confirms this. Chapter 70 on the list can be larger than line item 7061-0008, because part of it
  is paid from other line items.

## Per-resident figures

Some amounts are also shown per resident: the amount divided by the number of people living in
Massachusetts. The population is the Census Bureau's estimate for July 1 at the start of the
fiscal year ([Vintage 2025 state estimates](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/datasets/2020-2025/state/totals/NST-EST2025-ALLDATA.csv)):
7,073,479 for FY2024, 7,138,560 for FY2025 and 7,154,084 for FY2026. FY2027 uses the FY2026
figure until a newer estimate is published.

This is an average, not what any person pays or receives. Much of the money comes from businesses
and the federal government, and spending reaches people very unevenly. For a year in progress,
the figure covers spending so far.

## Highlight lens

The **Highlight** control lets a reader pick any spending areas or departments and see them
highlighted across the Flow, Scale and Changes views, with a subtotal. The selection lives in
the page URL (`?hl=sec:EEA,dept:EHS/DMH`) so a highlighted view can be shared. The site ships no
preset selections and takes no position on what should be highlighted.

The data files carry one derived flag, `conservation: true`, on the Energy & Environmental
Affairs secretariat (CTHRU `ENVIRONMENTAL AFFAIRS`) and on the Department of Conservation &
Recreation (`DCR`) and the Department of Fish & Game (`FWE`). It is a mechanical label kept for
anyone reusing the data; the site's interface does not read it.

## Who explains the changes

This site shows what changed and by how much, not why. These sources explain the reasons. Each
has its own point of view, so reading more than one gives a fuller picture.

- **The governor's budget summary** ([budget.digital.mass.gov/govbudget](https://budget.digital.mass.gov/govbudget/fy27/)):
  the administration's explanation of its proposal.
- **House and Senate Ways & Means** ([malegislature.gov/Budget](https://malegislature.gov/Budget)):
  each chamber's budget, amendments and executive summary, in its own words.
- **Massachusetts Budget and Policy Center** ([massbudget.org](https://massbudget.org/)):
  research group whose analyses generally favor public investment and focus on low- and
  middle-income residents.
- **Massachusetts Taxpayers Foundation** ([masstaxpayers.org](https://masstaxpayers.org/)):
  business-supported research group whose analyses focus on fiscal discipline and the state's
  long-term finances.
- **Pioneer Institute** ([pioneerinstitute.org](https://pioneerinstitute.org/)): free-market
  think tank that publishes budget and spending reports.
- **News coverage:** State House News Service ([statehousenews.com](https://www.statehousenews.com/),
  by subscription) follows the budget most closely; CommonWealth Beacon
  ([commonwealthbeacon.org](https://commonwealthbeacon.org/)) covers state policy as a nonprofit
  newsroom.

Open Ledger MA is not affiliated with any of them, and listing a source is not an endorsement.

## Known limitations

- **FY2027 is in progress.** Spending covers activity recorded through the date shown in
  `meta.json` (`partial.2027.through`); revenue for FY2027 covers fiscal periods through
  September 2026 (`partial.2027.revenueThrough`). Year-over-year comparisons with FY2027
  compare a partial year with full years. FY2027 revenue so far also has a different mix by
  source than a full year, because some revenue (April income tax payments, many fund transfers)
  arrives late in the year.
- **Budget comparisons with a year in progress.** A closed year's "Budgeted" figure includes the
  supplemental budgets passed during it; FY2027 so far has only its original GAA. The Changes view
  says so and gives the GAA-to-GAA change, and marks programs with $0 so far as "none yet"
  rather than "ended".
- **Amounts are nominal.** All figures are in dollars of each year, not adjusted for inflation.
- **Closed years can still move slightly.** CTHRU is updated daily; late adjustments to FY2026
  can change totals after the fact. The weekly refresh picks them up.
- **Supplemental budgets** are included in "Budgeted" when CTHRU has recorded them. Supplemental
  budgets passed after a pull are not reflected until the next weekly refresh.
- **Off-budget trusts and non-budgeted funds** are outside this picture. The spending dataset
  covers appropriated accounts and other spending authorizations recorded in the state
  accounting system; the revenue side is limited to budgeted funds. Capital (bond-funded)
  spending is shown beside the charts but not in them (see "Capital spending"). Most trust funds, and authorities such as the MBTA, Massachusetts Port Authority,
  and the Massachusetts School Building Authority spend money that does not appear here except
  where the state budget transfers money to them.
- **Capital is payments, not appropriations.** There is no single annual capital budget to
  compare with, so the capital figure is what was paid out in the year, in both the Budgeted and
  Spent views.
- **Federal pass-through.** Federal reimbursements (mostly for MassHealth) are counted as
  revenue when received, and the related spending is counted in the programs that spend it.
  Federal grants spent through federal trust funds are generally not in these datasets.
- **Pandemic-era federal funds** (Federal COVID-19 Response Fund) appear in the spending data
  for some accounts and years.
- **Naming drift.** CTHRU account names change and are truncated at about 40 characters.
  Programs are matched by account number. When a new account number replaces an old one, the
  two appear as separate programs in the Changes view (one going to $0, one starting from $0).
- **Revenue and spending totals do not reconcile, and are not forced to.** The two datasets
  have different scopes (spending includes other statutory authorizations and intragovernmental
  charges; revenue includes transfers between funds) and different timing. The site shows both
  totals as reported. In the Flow view, revenue node labels show the amount collected, while the
  bands are scaled to total spending.
- **Rounding.** Values are rounded to whole dollars at the program level; departments,
  spending areas and the statewide total are exact sums of the rounded programs, so they can differ
  from a raw CTHRU sum by a few dollars.

## Reproducing the data

```sh
make setup   # Python 3.12+ virtualenv with requests and pandas
make pull    # download raw rows to etl/raw/ (gitignored); FORCE=1 to refresh
make build   # write data/*.json and the totals table above
make check   # verify totals within 0.5% and that every view's numbers add up
```

Set `SOCRATA_APP_TOKEN` in the environment to send a Socrata app token (optional).
